# Travel

## Who's watching
People planning a trip, or daydreaming about one. They save travel videos into trip folders and come back to them the week before they fly. They want to avoid wasting a day, money or an experience on the wrong choice, and to feel like they know something other tourists don't.

## What counts as a specific
Every item or beat gives the viewer at least one of these:
- a named place, and exactly what to do there
- timing: the time of day, day of week or season that changes the experience
- logistics: how to get there, what to book ahead, which entrance, what to bring
- the trade-off: what you give up by choosing it (crowds, cost, distance)
- a mistake tourists make and what to do instead

These are not specifics: "stunning views", "breathtaking", "rich culture", "something for everyone", "a must-see", "vibrant". If a line could appear in any city's brochure, cut it.

## Formats that work
- **Tourist mistakes.** What first-timers get wrong and the fix for each.
- **Ranked list.** Commit to an order and say why #1 wins. Fit the list to the runtime: at most 3 items at 30s, 4 at 45s, 5 at 60s. If the topic promises more, give "my top 3".
- **Skip this, do this.** The famous option against the better one, with the reason.
- **How to actually do it.** Timing and logistics for one famous thing.
- **Story.** One moment on the trip that taught a lesson the viewer can use.

## Hooks that work here
- A contrarian verdict: "The best view of the Eiffel Tower is the one where you can see it."
- A mistake with a cost: "Most people waste their first morning in Rome in a queue they didn't need."
- A timing secret: "Go to the Louvre on the one evening most tourists don't know about."
- A moment already in motion: "I missed the last train in Tokyo at 12:15, and it was my fault."

## Banned phrases
- stunning views
- breathtaking
- wanderlust
- bucket list
- off the beaten path
- rich culture
- vibrant
- nestled
- paradise
- a must-see

## Pitfalls
- Listing famous places without saying what to do there or when.
- Hedging ("it depends what kind of traveller you are") instead of recommending.
- Brochure language instead of what the viewer will actually see, hear or pay.
- Inventing facts: prices, opening hours, ticket rules or distances you aren't sure of. Stick to well-known facts about real places. Opinions ("skip it", "the best") are fine; made-up facts are not.

## Closes that work
- "Save this for your [city] trip."
- "Send this to who you're travelling with."
- A question that invites a local tip: "What would you add for a first-timer?"

## Editor checks
- Does every item tell the viewer what to do, when, or what to avoid?
- Is there at least one detail only someone who has been would know?
- Is any line brochure copy that could describe any city?

## Example scripts
These show the level of detail to match. Never reuse their content or phrasing.

### Tourist mistakes · 45s · conversational
**Hook:** Three things that trip up almost every first-timer in Tokyo.

**Body:** First, the bins. There are almost no public trash cans, so you'll be carrying that onigiri wrapper for an hour. Keep a small bag in your pocket and use the bins inside convenience stores. Second, the trains stop. Most lines finish around midnight, and a taxi across the city costs far more than you'd expect. Check your last train before your second drink, not after. Third, cash. Cards work in most big places, but small ramen counters and ticket machines still want coins and notes. Keep a few thousand yen on you, and get an IC card so you can tap through the gates.

**CTA:** Save this for your first Tokyo trip.

### Story · 30s · storytelling
**Hook:** I checked in online and still almost missed my flight.

**Body:** I strolled into the airport forty minutes before departure, feeling organised. Boarding pass on my phone. Then I saw the bag drop sign. It closed an hour before the flight, and my suitcase was going nowhere without me. Checking in online doesn't mean you can skip the desk. If you have a bag to check, the deadline is the bag drop, not boarding.

**CTA:** Send this to the friend who always arrives late.
