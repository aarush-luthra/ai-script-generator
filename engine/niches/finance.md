# Personal finance

## Who's watching
People who feel behind with money and want one thing they can do this week. Many are in their twenties or thirties, living paycheck to paycheck or just starting to save. They distrust hype and get-rich content. They save videos they plan to act on, and they want permission to start small.

## What counts as a specific
Every item or beat gives the viewer at least one of these:
- an exact action: open this kind of account, set this transfer, change this setting
- a number to use: an amount, a percentage, a date, a timeframe
- the mechanism: why it works, in one plain sentence
- a before-and-after the viewer can picture
- the mistake, and what it costs

These are not specifics: "take control of your finances", "financial freedom", "build wealth", "be smart with money", "make your money work for you". If a line has no action and no number, cut it.

## Formats that work
- **One move, explained.** A single action, the exact steps and why it works.
- **Myth vs reality.** A common belief that costs people money, corrected.
- **Story.** A personal before-and-after with real, modest numbers the creator can swap for their own.
- **The mistake.** What most people do wrong with a common product (credit cards, savings accounts, pay rises).
- **Ranked list.** Commit to an order: "do this first" and why. At most 3 items at 30s, 4 at 45s, 5 at 60s.

## Hooks that work here
- A contrarian claim: "Carrying a credit card balance doesn't help your credit score."
- A painless-change promise with a stake: "The savings account you forget about beats the one you check every day."
- A moment in a story: "I opened my banking app and found money I didn't remember saving."
- A mistake with a cost: "Your pay rise disappears in three months unless you do this on day one."

## Banned phrases
- financial freedom
- build wealth
- passive income
- make your money work for you
- take control of your finances
- get rich
- millionaire
- financial journey

## Pitfalls
- Vague advice ("budget better", "save more") with no amount, account or step.
- Hype, promises of returns, or anything that sounds like a get-rich scheme.
- Specific investment picks or guaranteed outcomes. Explain mechanisms, not predictions.
- Invented statistics about "most people". Personal story numbers should be modest and plausible, so the creator can swap in their own.

## Closes that work
- "Save this for payday."
- A question that gets people to commit: "What amount could you automate without noticing?"
- "Follow for the next step once this is set up."

## Editor checks
- Could a viewer do the main action today, from the script alone?
- Is there at least one real number?
- Does anything sound like hype or a promise of returns?

## Example scripts
These show the level of detail to match. Never reuse their content or phrasing.

### One move, explained · 45s · calm expert
**Hook:** An emergency fund starts with one account you can't see from your main app.

**Body:** Open a savings account at a different bank from your current account. That small bit of friction matters: money you can see in two taps gets spent. Then set a transfer for the day your pay lands, not the end of the month, when there's nothing left. Start with an amount you won't miss, even twenty-five a week. Your first target isn't six months of expenses. It's one month of essentials: rent, bills, food, transport. Hit that, and a surprise car repair becomes an inconvenience instead of a debt.

**CTA:** Save this for your next payday.

### Myth vs reality · 30s · blunt
**Hook:** Carrying a balance on your credit card does nothing for your score.

**Body:** People leave a little unpaid each month to look like good borrowers. Your score doesn't reward that. What it tracks is whether you pay on time and how much of your limit you use. Pay the full statement balance every month. You get the same credit history and pay zero interest. Carrying a balance only helps the bank.

**CTA:** Send this to the friend who thinks it helps.
