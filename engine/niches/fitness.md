# Fitness

## Who's watching
People who already train, or are trying to, and feel stuck or confused by conflicting advice. They're sceptical of influencers selling programmes. They want one change to make in their next session and a way to tell whether it's working.

## What counts as a specific
Every item or beat gives the viewer at least one of these:
- sets, reps, weight change, rest time or frequency
- a measurable signal: what to track and what counts as progress
- a cue: exactly what to do with the body in one movement
- the reason it works, in one plain sentence
- the mistake and what it looks like from the inside ("your warm-up feels heavy")

These are not specifics: "push yourself", "listen to your body", "stay consistent", "get after it", "results". If a line doesn't change what the viewer does in the gym, cut it.

## Formats that work
- **Myth vs reality.** A belief that keeps people stuck, corrected.
- **The mistake.** What it feels like, why it happens and the fix.
- **One rule, explained.** A simple system with numbers (double progression, rest times).
- **Test or experiment.** "I tried X for four weeks": what changed, with numbers.
- **Ranked list.** Commit to an order and justify #1. At most 3 items at 30s, 4 at 45s, 5 at 60s.

## Hooks that work here
- A blunt reframe: "Being sore the next day doesn't mean the workout worked."
- A symptom the viewer recognises: "If your warm-up sets feel heavy, you're not training too little."
- A contrarian rule: "Stop adding weight every week."
- A tension with effort: "You're training hard enough. That's the problem."

## Banned phrases
- no pain no gain
- beast mode
- shred
- toned
- get after it
- crush your goals
- fitness journey
- burn fat fast
- secret exercise

## Pitfalls
- Motivation instead of instruction.
- No numbers: advice without sets, reps, weight or a way to measure progress.
- Medical or injury claims, or promises about fat loss in a timeframe.
- Absolutes about complex topics. Say what works for most people training normally.

## Closes that work
- "Save this for your next leg day."
- A question about their own training: "Which lift has been stuck longest?"
- "Try it for four sessions and tell me what changed."

## Editor checks
- Does the viewer know exactly what to change in their next session?
- Is there at least one number (sets, reps, weight, rest, weeks)?
- Is any line motivation rather than instruction?

## Example scripts
These show the level of detail to match. Never reuse their content or phrasing.

### One rule, explained · 45s · calm expert
**Hook:** You don't need to add weight every week to keep getting stronger.

**Body:** Use a rep range instead. Pick one, say eight to twelve, and choose a weight you can lift for eight clean reps on every set. Each session, try to add a rep or two. Same weight, more reps. When you hit twelve on every set, add the smallest jump the gym allows and drop back to eight. That's double progression. It works because reps are easier to add than plates. And it gives you a clear signal: if your reps stall for three sessions, look at sleep and recovery, not effort.

**CTA:** Save this and try it on your next three sessions.

### Myth vs reality · 30s · blunt
**Hook:** Being sore the next day doesn't mean the workout worked.

**Body:** Soreness mostly means you did something new. That's why your first leg day wrecks you and your tenth barely does, even though you're lifting more. So stop chasing the ache. Judge the session by your logbook instead. More reps or more weight than last time is progress, sore or not.

**CTA:** Comment the lift you're going to track this week.
