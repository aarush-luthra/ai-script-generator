# Food & restaurants

## Who's watching
People deciding where to eat and what to order, usually this week and usually with someone else. They save food videos as a to-do list and send them to the person they're eating with. They want to walk in knowing exactly what to do.

## What counts as a specific
Every item or beat gives the viewer at least one of these:
- the exact dish or drink to order, by its menu name
- how to order it: ask for X, get it Y way, skip Z
- when to go or how to beat the wait: a day, a time, a reservation trick
- what's actually on the plate: ingredients, texture, how it's cooked
- a price, only if you are confident it's right

These are not specifics: "delicious", "amazing", "authentic", "packed with flavour", "the vibes", "a must-try", "worth every penny". Saying a taco shop "is for tacos" is a restatement, not information. If a line could be written without ever having eaten there, cut it.

## Formats that work
- **Ranked list.** Commit to an order and say why #1 beats #2: the one dish nobody else does, value, consistency. Fit the list to the runtime: at most 3 items at 30s, 4 at 45s, 5 at 60s. If the topic promises more ("top 10"), give "my top 3" and own it, rather than rushing through names.
- **Order this, not that.** One place, the dish everyone gets versus the one to get.
- **The mistake.** What most people order wrong, or the trap on the menu.
- **How to order.** A walk-through for an intimidating menu or format.
- **Worth the hype?** A clear verdict with the reason, not "it depends".

## Hooks that work here
- A verdict with a stake: "The best thing at a Neapolitan pizzeria isn't the margherita."
- A mistake the viewer is probably making: "You're ordering Korean barbecue in the wrong order."
- An order instruction that creates curiosity: "Walk past the burger and order the thing on the chalkboard."
- A tension between hype and reality: "The two-hour line is for the wrong dish."

## Banned phrases
- hidden gem
- foodie
- mouthwatering
- taste buds
- to die for
- hits different
- flavor explosion
- flavour explosion
- melt in your mouth
- you won't believe

## Pitfalls
- Describing taste with adjectives instead of saying what's on the plate.
- Hedging ("depends what you're in the mood for") instead of recommending.
- Naming places without telling the viewer what to order at each.
- Inventing facts about real places: prices, hours, chef names, or dishes you aren't sure they serve. For a real restaurant, stick to what it is well known for. Opinions ("the best", "skip it") are fine; made-up facts are not.

## Closes that work
- "Send this to whoever you're eating with."
- "Save this for your next trip to [city]."
- A question that forces a pick: "Which one would you queue for?"

## Editor checks
- Does every item tell the viewer what to order or do?
- In a ranking, is there a clear #1 with a reason it beats the rest?
- Is there a single line that describes taste with adjectives only?

## Example scripts
These show the level of detail to match. Never reuse their content or phrasing.

### How to order · 45s · conversational
**Hook:** You're ordering Korean barbecue in the wrong order, and your grill pays for it.

**Body:** Most tables start with galbi, the sweet marinated short rib. That sugar chars fast and coats the grill, so everything after it tastes burnt. Start plain instead. Pork belly or thin-sliced brisket, just salt, cooked while the grill is still clean. Then move to the marinated cuts. And go easy on the refillable sides before the meat lands. The kimchi and pickled radish are there to cut the fat, so save them for the pork belly. Finish with a shared stew or cold noodles once the grill has done its job.

**CTA:** Send this to whoever always orders the galbi first.

### Ranked list · 30s · energetic
**Hook:** Skip the pepperoni at a Neapolitan pizzeria and order these instead.

**Body:** Number three: the marinara. No cheese. Just tomato, garlic, oregano and oil, so you find out fast if the dough is good. Number two: the margherita, the pizza every place gets judged on. Number one: whatever's on the day's special board, because that's where the kitchen shows off. Eat it fast. That soft, wet centre is on purpose, and it sets as it cools.

**CTA:** Tag the friend who orders pepperoni everywhere.
