# why your pay rise disappears within three months

**Niche:** Personal finance · **Target:** 45s · **Tone:** calm expert · **Model:** gpt-6-luna

## Script

**[0:00–0:05] HOOK**

The treat you remember isn't the problem; the monthly bill you forgot is.

**[0:05–0:45] BODY**

The dinner or phone upgrade gets blamed because you remember buying it. But that cost ends; a new monthly payment takes money every month.

A bigger paycheck can make higher rent, a car payment, or another subscription feel manageable. Each decision arrives separately, so the total is easy to miss.

After a few pay cycles, those bills are already spoken for. Compare your old and new fixed monthly costs with the actual increase in take-home pay.

For example, a $300 net raise and $260 in new bills leaves just $40 before food and one-off spending. That's where the raise went.

**[0:45–0:49] CTA**

Save this for payday before another monthly bill settles in.

## Directions considered

### 1. Myth vs reality ✅ chosen

- **Angle (Surprising):** The raise often feels gone not because of one expensive treat, but because recurring costs quietly reset around the larger paycheck.
- **Hook:** Your pay rise usually disappears through monthly commitments, not one big purchase.
- **Brief:** Contrast the tempting one-off-spending explanation with how recurring expenses absorb new take-home pay over a few pay cycles. Viewers learn to look for the monthly costs that keep repeating, not just the purchase they remember.

### 2. POV or story

- **Angle (Story):** Follow the raise through three paydays, showing how ordinary decisions—not a dramatic splurge—make it hard to notice.
- **Hook:** The raise hit my account—and by month three, I couldn't find it.
- **Brief:** Tell this as a specific, modest story using the creator’s real figures: the first payday, the new recurring expense, and the third-month balance. Viewers see how to trace a raise through their own account without blaming one memorable purchase.

### 3. Mini tutorial

- **Angle (Practical):** Give the raise a job before everyday spending adjusts, using the first payday as the decision point.
- **Hook:** Your raise has one safe window: before your monthly spending adjusts.
- **Brief:** Walk through a three-step payday setup that separates the increase from the account used for daily spending. Viewers leave with a concrete way to preserve some of the raise before new recurring costs take hold.

**Why chosen:** The contrast between a memorable splurge and quiet recurring costs creates a strong curiosity gap, then pays it off with a clear account check viewers can share.
